# English Language Paper 2 (Writing) — scoring rubric (2024+ format)

Status: **draft v0.1 (2026-10-07), awaiting review by an English teacher.**

How this rubric is sourced:
- **Official** parts are from free HKEAA documents, quoted with a `[key]` that refers to the sources table at the bottom.
- **Distilled** parts come from the HKEAA level exemplars (2024, 2025; exemplars 1–3 of each level). Each is cited like `2025 L4e2 B`, meaning 2025, Level 4, exemplar 2, Part B. Exemplar 4 is held out as test data and was not used.
- **Estimate** parts are our own conventions, used where HKEAA publishes nothing.

Scope: Part A and Part B, both scored.

---

## 1. Paper structure (official)

| | Part A | Part B |
|---|---|---|
| Share of subject | 10% | 15% |
| Task | One compulsory short, guided task (situation, purpose and information given), about 200 words. It may be split into sections, e.g. 150 + 50. | One of 4 questions, more open-ended, about 400 words |
| Time | 2 hours for both parts together | |

- Since 2024, Part B has 4 questions (previously 8), and they are **no longer linked to the electives**. The text types vary from year to year [SEM25 slide 31; QA24 Q4, Q8].
- Recent Part B tasks [PPT24 slide 69; PPT25 slide 66]:
  - 2024: essay, blog post, letter to the editor, school magazine article.
  - 2025: short story, letter to the editor, essay, blog post.

## 2. Marking (official, plus estimates)

- **Three domains: Content (C), Language (L), Organisation (O).** Each part is scored **out of 21** by two markers. The four scores are "combined, taking into consideration the relative weighting of each task" [FAQ-P2].
- **Part A and Part B use the same base guidelines**, which "may be adapted depending on the requirements of the task" [FAQ-P2].
- **Best fit:** markers choose the level in each domain that "on balance best describes the script" [FAQ-P2].
- **Marking is positive, with no deductions** [QA24 Q6].
- **Marks are not levels.** Cut scores are set by experts each year and vary [FAQ-P2].
- **Estimate: each domain is 0–7** (21 ÷ 3). This is consistent with official sources but not stated outright.
- **Estimate: paper score = 0.4 × A/21 + 0.6 × B/21** (the 10 : 15 weighting).
- **Not available free:** the full verbatim 0–7 descriptors for each mark point. They are in HKEAA's paid SRR Information Package (2009, pp.91–92) and the annual Question Papers booklets. *If the school has a copy, adding the descriptors here would be the single biggest improvement to this rubric.*

### Top-band criteria (verbatim, [SEM25] slides 32–34)

- **Content:**
  - fulfils the requirements of the question
  - relevant
  - ideas well developed/supported
  - creativity and imagination shown when appropriate
  - engages the reader's interest consistently, with a high awareness of audience
- **Language:**
  - wide range of accurate sentence structures, with a good grasp of more complex structures
  - grammar accurate (only very minor slips)
  - vocabulary well chosen, often expressing subtleties of meaning
  - spelling and punctuation almost entirely correct
  - register, tone and style entirely appropriate to the genre and text type
- **Organisation:**
  - text organised effectively
  - logical development of ideas
  - cohesion clear
  - cohesive ties sophisticated throughout
  - overall structure coherent, sophisticated and appropriate to the genre and text type

### Policies (official)

- **Length:**
  - The word count is a guide. Writing well under it "will inevitably fall short of fulfilling the task requirements" [FAQ-P2].
  - Length is not rewarded. Over-long, rambling texts and "low-frequency vocabulary" used as decoration don't help [QA24 Q6; PPT24 slide 74].
  - *Estimate:* no hard cap. Under about 60% of the word guide → flag it, and let it count against C.
- **Memorised answers:** "Any memorised model answers incorporated will be disregarded by markers" [HB26].
- **Real names:** not allowed; candidates should use aliases such as Chris Wong, Mary or Peter [HB26].
- **Off-topic or copied prompt text:** there is no official Paper 2 rule. *Estimate:* treat it as low C, discount copied prompt text, and flag it for the teacher; never auto-zero.

## 3. Level descriptors for writing (official, verbatim, [LDW])

| Level | Content | Language and style | Organisation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Relevant and extensive, aware of purpose, engages the reader's interest. Creativity and imagination shown when appropriate. | A wide range of sentence structures, accurate and appropriate. Accurate punctuation and grammar. Wide, appropriate vocabulary, with some more ambitious and sophisticated language. Register, tone and style appropriate to the text type. | Wholly coherent and appropriate to the genre. Effective paragraphing. Sophisticated cohesion. |
| 4 | Relevant, detailed in parts, engages the reader's interest. Creativity in most parts. | A range of structures, accurate and appropriate. Errors do not affect overall clarity. Moderately wide vocabulary, mostly spelt correctly. Register mostly appropriate. | Coherent in most parts. Paragraphing sufficient for overall coherence. Cohesion between most sentences successful. |
| 3 | Most content relevant. Several examples of creativity. | Simple and some complex sentences well formed. Basic punctuation and some basic grammar accurate. Common vocabulary appropriate and spelt correctly. Some appropriate register. | Some sections coherent. Paragraphing effective in parts. Cohesion between some sentences successful. |
| 2 | Some relevant content. Some features of familiar genres. | Simple sentences well formed. Grammar sufficient for some sentences to be comprehensible. Simple vocabulary mostly correct. | Structure discernible when the genre is simple and familiar. Some paragraphing. Simple links. |
| 1 | A few relevant points. | A few simple comprehensible sentences. A few examples of simple vocabulary used appropriately. | A few links between sentences. |

There are no published descriptors for 5* or 5**, and the exemplars cover Levels 1–5 only.

## 4. Level markers (distilled from the exemplars)

**The most important finding:** at the top levels, *range and control of purpose* matter more than accuracy. Level 5 scripts can still contain frequent errors (2024 L5e1 B; 2025 L5e2 B: "I fervently believes", "we will failed"), as long as the errors never block meaning. Going down the levels, what changes is **whether errors reach basic verb forms and start to hide meaning**.

### Level 5 (vs 4)
- **C — every point developed.**
  - Each input point is covered, with a consequence and a concrete remedy. Writers invent stakes-raising context, such as an elderly mother on Mother's Day (2024 L5e3 A) or a beef allergy (2024 L5e1 A).
  - Part B uses specific examples (named artists and songs, reports, statistics) and handles counter-arguments with concession and rebuttal (2024 L5e1 B, L5e2 B; 2025 L5e2 B).
  - Two-part prompts are balanced, with each solution matched to a challenge (2025 L5e3 B).
  - Stories are crafted with a frame, a motif and a circular ending (2025 L5e1 B). Conclusions reframe the argument: "Only we humans can." (2024 L5e3 B).
- **L — precise vocabulary and natural variety.**
  - Precise, idiomatic vocabulary: "drastic disparity", "pungent smell … permeated", "tantamount to", "diluted responsibilities".
  - Varied structures used naturally: participle openers, inversion, short sentences for effect.
  - Errors are local slips: subject–verb agreement with long noun phrases, a passive missing its auxiliary, which/who, verb patterns, dangling modifiers.
- **O — transitions that carry the argument.** "Our disappointment does not stop there." (2024 L5e3 A). Conventions are fully handled: headings plus a call to action, the letter frame, an essay thesis, paragraphed dialogue. Organisation alone doesn't separate Level 5 from Level 4 (2024 L5e1 B is formulaic).

### Level 4 (vs 5 / 3)
- **C — every part addressed, but unevenly.** Some sections are summarised rather than illustrated (2025 L4e1 A, L4e3 A). Some content is padded or generic, e.g. an encyclopaedia-style explanation of Instagram (2024 L4e2 B) or invented statistics with muddled logic (2024 L4e1 B). There is still a real anecdote, and the persona is adopted (2025 L4e3 B, Head of HR).
- **L — errors reach basic verb forms but don't hide meaning.**
  - Verb-form errors: "was not work", "without apologised", "I suggest you carrying out" (2024 L4e1 A, L4e2 A).
  - Wrong words: inhibit for prohibit; intolerant for intolerable.
  - **Memorised formal phrases clash with plain content:** "Divergent as the arguments are…" next to "this is quite upset" (2024 L4e3 B). Idioms misfire: "peaked the interest", "strike a chord with everyone".
  - Narratives switch tense (2025 L4e1 B), and register slips (2025 L4e2 B).
- **O — conventional but mechanical.** Coherent overall, with mechanical sentence-initial connectors (Also / Moreover / However), as noted in the examiner comment [PPT24]. Comma splices weaken cohesion, and some conventions are immature, such as ending a story with "THE END" (2025 L4e1 B).

### Level 3 (vs 4 / 2)
- **C — points are generic and sometimes self-defeating.**
  - A complaint letter praises room service, or asks for nothing (2024 L3e1 A, L3e3 A).
  - The persona slips: "even I am a successful musician" (2024 L3e2 B).
  - Content drifts off topic (2024 L3e3 B, L3e1 B).
  - Elements are only loosely met: a second-hand "member's experience" (2025 L3e2 A, L3e3 A); a letter that gives reasons but no suggestions (2025 L3e2 B).
  - Above Level 2 because every paragraph still has a clear, relevant point.
- **L — errors sometimes hide meaning.**
  - Passive used with intransitive verbs: "are suffered from".
  - -ed/-ing confusion: "It was disappointed".
  - Dummy subjects: "there would provide".
  - Missing "be": "I still scared".
  - Double verbs: "was get hurt".
  - Plurals of uncountable nouns: feedbacks, researches.
  - Double subjects.
  - **Memorised "upgrade" chunks set against weak grammar:** "Only in this way can…", "Without further ado" placed at the end (2024 L3e1; 2025 L3e2).
- **O — one paragraph per point, but logic jumps inside paragraphs.**
  - Connectors are misused: "On the other hand" used to add a point; "Therefore" four times (2025 L3e3 B).
  - Formulaic endings.
  - Conventions only partly handled, e.g. no letter close (2025 L3e2 B).

### Level 2 (vs 3 / 1)
- **C — few points, little support.**
  - Fewer points (two in 2024 L2e1 A), listed with little support. Elements are misread (2025 L2e1 A).
  - **Wrong tone for the task:** threatening a "one star comment" (2024 L2e3 A); calling non-buyers "devil" (2025 L2e2 B).
  - Part B loops or slides into "you" advice.
  - Above Level 1 because the main thread can still be followed.
- **L — errors in most sentences, including simple ones.**
  - Modal + wrong verb form: "will reposted", "can't saw".
  - Two main verbs fused in one clause: "This makes social media is the best way".
  - Dropped "be". Basic agreement errors: "it have".
  - Pronoun case: "keep touch with she".
  - Double comparatives: "more bigger".
  - Misspelled key words: hotal, webste.
  - Ambitious vocabulary does **not** lift a script out of Level 2 (2025 L2e2).
- **O — paragraphs and sequencers survive, but conventions break.**
  - Dear Sir/Madam paired with "Yours sincerely"; no sign-off; signed with the wrong persona (2024 L2e1 A, L2e2 A, L2e3 A/B).
  - Conclusions introduce new ideas.
  - Comma-spliced chains, and heavy deletions that show weak planning.

### Level 1 (vs 2)
- **C — off task, or only fragments relevant.**
  - Part A may still name 3–4 relevant points (2024 L1e1 A, L1e3 A).
  - Part B is off task or barely there: an autobiography instead of advice (2024 L1e2 B), one paragraph that breaks off (2024 L1e1 B), design advice instead of leaflet content (2025 L1e2 A), the prompt copied as the intro (2025 L1e3 B).
  - Inappropriate openings: "Hello,", "sorry to waste your time".
- **L — the reader has to reconstruct meaning.**
  - Chinese patterns carried over directly: "There have a huge building", "I no friend", "I was went".
  - Wrong words destroy sense: "bad" for bed; "well" for will.
  - Fragments are the norm.
- **O — only a skeleton.** One- or two-sentence paragraphs under Firstly / Secondly / Lastly. A memorised list of linkers pasted over the content regardless of fit (2025 L1e2 A/B). Random capitalisation, and circular conclusions.

## 5. Error tags (feedback taxonomy)

These tags drive the student's weakness profile (`error_tag_stats`). Each tag is listed with the levels where it is typical.

| Tag | Example | Typical levels |
|---|---|---|
| `en.sva` (subject–verb agreement) | "I fervently believes" | L5 occasional → L3–L1 pervasive |
| `en.verb_form` | "we will failed", "was get hurt", "can be accept" | all levels; it's the key L4/L3 boundary |
| `en.tense_consistency` | tense switching in narratives | the hallmark of L4 stories; L3 and below |
| `en.tense_choice` | "I have joined … last month" | L4 and below |
| `en.passive` | "the view will not blocked", "are suffered from" | L5–L3 |
| `en.missing_be` | "I still scared", "hotel service also bad" | L3–L1 |
| `en.double_verb` | "This makes social media is…" | L3–L2 |
| `en.article` | "a employee", "a AI" | L4–L2 |
| `en.count_noun` | advices, feedbacks, researches, "a staff" | L5–L2 |
| `en.relative_clause` | "a student which have" | L5–L3 |
| `en.pronoun_case` | "help they know" | L2–L1 |
| `en.preposition_pattern` | "look forward for", "suggest to build" | all levels |
| `en.word_form` | disappointed vs disappointing, "After pregnant" | L4–L2 |
| `en.word_choice` | inhibit for prohibit | all levels |
| `en.misused_idiom` | "strike a chord with everyone", "Without further ado" at the end | L4–L2 |

| `en.run_on` | comma splices, "and/because" chains | L4 and below |
| `en.fragment` | | L3–L1 |
| `en.double_subject` | "The manager after knowing this, he…" | L3–L2 |
| `en.comparative` | "more bigger" | L2–L1 |
| `en.dangling_modifier` | | L5 |
| `en.spelling` | hotal, webste | L3 and below |

## 6. How the scorer uses this (estimate)

1. Part A and Part B are scored separately: C, L and O, each 0–7, best fit.
2. Retrieve **calibration anchors**: one exemplar per level for the same part, preferring the same text type, from the anchor set only.
3. Give each domain mark a reason that names the §4 markers it observed and the anchor it was compared with.
4. Tag errors with the §5 taxonomy. The density and type of errors inform L.
5. Overall level: estimated from both parts weighted 2 : 3, then calibrated on the held-out exemplars. HKEAA gives **one level per script**, not per part.
6. Everything is shown as an estimate, and the teacher's grade overrides it.

1. The full 0–7 descriptors are paid material. If the school owns the HKEAA publication, adding them would improve this rubric most.
2. The 0–7 scale per domain, the paper formula and the mapping from marks to level are estimates.
